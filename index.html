Algebra & finite fields

Shujun Zhao

Ph.D. candidate in Mathematics

University of South Florida

My research interests include finite fields, algebraic function fields, and polynomials and rational functions over finite fields, with applications to coding theory and cryptography. My advisor is Prof. Xiang-dong Hou.

Research interests

I.

Sum-free functions and cryptography

I study higher-order sum-freedom, which generalizes almost perfect nonlinearity (APN), with a focus on the binary multiplicative inverse function, Welch functions, and power functions of algebraic degree three.

Recent work ↗
II.

Rational functions over finite fields

With collaborators, I complete the classification of degree-three rational functions over finite fields by treating odd characteristic, under the action of projective linear groups by pre- and post-composition.

Classification paper ↗
III.

Linearized polynomials and computation

With collaborators, I use the theory of Drinfeld modules to develop a faster algorithm for computing characteristic polynomials of linear maps represented by linearized polynomials of fixed q-degree over large finite field extensions.

Classification of rational functions of degree three over finite fields ↗

Xiang-dong Hou, Siyu Peng, Yongyu Qiang, and Shujun Zhao

arXiv:2604.18954

arXiv ↗PDF ↗
About this paper

We classify degree-three rational functions over finite fields of odd characteristic up to PGL-equivalence. The classification uses value frequencies, ramification points, and an explicit count of equivalence classes.

Teaching

I want students to develop both the technical skills needed to solve problems and the confidence to ask why a method works, recognize its limitations, and apply it in unfamiliar settings.

In Calculus II, I emphasize connections between methods, the conditions under which formulas apply, and deliver clear explanations of each step. I support students' independent study by regularly posting organized lecture notes, provide exam review materials and lead review sessions to help students organize the main ideas and identify connections among topics.
